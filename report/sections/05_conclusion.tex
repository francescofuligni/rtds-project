\section{Conclusion}
% Reflect on the contribution, limitations and future work.

Together, the two studies show that the average reconstruction error gives an incomplete
picture of what a Rashomon release exposes. Known attributes add little, the number of
released trees and the way they are selected matter much more, and the attacker can tell
which reconstructed records are most likely correct. Rules for publishing models should
therefore also consider how trees are selected and which records become nearly certain.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Our results come from small samples of the Adult data with a coarse
  binary encoding, known income labels and public data from the same population. Only 640
  different records are possible, and public statistics alone already get 58.5\% of the claims right. The confidence study uses
  20 seeds and no background knowledge, and some returned tables are not proven to be the
  closest ones, although doubling the solve time and workers on ten seeds changed accuracy by
  only 0.1 points. The smoothing of the public-statistics control was chosen
  after looking at its calibration, which favours it. Finally, we could not confirm that the current data
  cache matches the one used for the original runs.

\paragraph{Future work.} Next steps are to test the confidence attack with background
  knowledge, on larger tables with a finer encoding, and with public data from a different
  population. Combining the two studies, with the same background knowledge given to the control
  without trees, would show whether known attributes make the
  records more reliable.
