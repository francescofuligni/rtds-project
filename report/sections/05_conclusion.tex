\section{Conclusion}
% Reflect on the contribution, limitations and future work.

Together, the two studies show why average reconstruction error gives an incomplete view
of a Rashomon release. The tested background attributes add modest gains, while tree
count and selection policy have larger effects. Public-guided confidence also reveals
which full-record claims are more reliable. Disclosure policies should therefore
consider both release policy and selective recovery.

\paragraph{Limitations.} These results concern small, coarsely encoded Adult samples,
  known income labels and public data from the same population. Only 640 encoded
  profiles, including income, are possible, and the public-only control already reaches
  58.5\% claim accuracy. Correct claims can therefore reflect common population
  profiles, making the improvement over this control essential to interpreting leakage.
  Main controls do not
  use A/B-conditioned imputation. Sex error uses evaluator-assisted matching, while
  confidence evaluates profile counts rather than personal identities. The confidence
  study uses 20 seeds and no background knowledge, and some candidate tables are not
  proven optima. A limited budget check provides some reassurance: with 200 public
  records and the Dirichlet prior at $m=25$, doubling solve time and workers on ten
  paired seeds changed accuracy from 68.4\% to 68.5\%. This check does not establish
  optimality or show that all settings are insensitive to the budget.
  Baseline smoothing was chosen after inspecting calibration, favouring
  that control. We could not confirm that the current data cache matches the one used
  for the original runs, which limits exact reproduction.

\paragraph{Future work.} Test confidence with background knowledge, larger tables
  and public data from a different population. Combining the two studies would show
  whether known attributes improve the reliability of full-record claims as well as
  sex reconstruction. Such comparisons should also give the no-tree control the same
  background information, so that the comparison measures what the released
  models add beyond background knowledge. Testing less coarse feature encodings would help assess whether the
  selective recovery observed here extends beyond this small profile space.
