\section{Introduction}
% Motivate the area and synthesize the individual literature reviews.

Interpretable models are recommended for high-stakes decisions \citep{rudin2019stop},
and publishing them can support transparency. Yet a decision tree published with the
class counts in every leaf is also a small table of statistics about its training data,
and statistics can be turned back into records. Census tables have been used to
reconstruct census microdata at national scale \citep{abowd2025census}, and reconstructed
census rows can be ranked by how often they recur across reconstructions \citep{dick2023confidence}. For
trees, \citet{ferry2024probabilistic} measure the uncertainty across all datasets
that fit one published tree, DRAFT \citep{ferry2024draft} reconstructs the training set
of a random forest with constraint programming, and \citet{gorge2026dp} extend it to
differentially private forests. \citet{hsu2026rashomon} show that publishing many
near-optimal models from one Rashomon set, which TreeFARMS \citep{xin2022treefarms}
enumerates for sparse trees, gives an attacker several views of the same data: their
DRAFT-based error mostly falls as more trees are released.

Our two individual literature reviews approached this work from different angles. The
first (De Coster) compared what reconstruction attacks output. Most of the reviewed attacks return one dataset:
apart from the single-model analysis of \citet{ferry2024probabilistic}, none attaches a
probability to each reconstructed value, and \citet{dick2023confidence} rank their rows
but state that the ranking is not calibrated. The second (Fuligni) studied the
attacker's background knowledge. DRAFT's experiments report reconstruction error as the number of known columns
increases \citep{ferry2024draft}. In a different setting,
\citet{mehnaz2022sensitive} vary which non-sensitive attributes are unknown in
black-box attribute inference. Both
reviews found the same evaluation weaknesses. Attacks on tree models are mostly compared
with uniform guessing, while a baseline that knows the data distribution can change the
conclusion \citep{abowd2025census,jayaraman2022imputation}, while \citet{wen2025reconstruction} distinguish reconstruction fidelity from coverage
in vision attacks. In our tabular setting, an additional concern is that matching an
anonymous reconstruction to true rows uses evaluator-only information. Our shared starting point is therefore the Rashomon
release of \citet{hsu2026rashomon} with a DRAFT-style attack and informed baselines, with
the questions identified in our individual reviews. Code, data and results
are available at \url{https://github.com/francescofuligni/rtds-project}.
