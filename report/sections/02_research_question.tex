\section{Research Problem and Question}
% Define the gap and state the research question and sub-questions.

A single average reconstruction error does not show how much the result depends on what the
attacker already knows, or which individual records are exposed. Our research question is
therefore: \emph{when $m$ sparse decision trees from one Rashomon set are published together
with their leaf class counts, how well can an attacker reconstruct the sex of the training
records, and can the attacker tell which reconstructed records are correct?} 
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item[SQ1.] How much does it help the attacker to know the marital status, education or age
      of every training record, compared with simply releasing more trees?
  \item[SQ2.] Does it matter which trees of the Rashomon set are released? We compare a
      random selection with the first $m$ trees in the order in which TreeFARMS lists them.
  \item[SQ3.] Using only the released trees and a public sample of the population, can the
      attacker estimate how reliable each reconstructed record is, and how much public data is needed?
\end{description}
SQ2 builds on \citet{hsu2026rashomon}, who limit how many trees are released as a defence.
We do not propose a new attack: we adapt DRAFT and, for SQ3, solve it repeatedly with
guidance from public data, similar to \citet{dick2023confidence}.
