\section{Introduction}
% Motivate the area and synthesize the individual literature reviews.

Interpretable models are recommended for high-stakes decisions \citep{rudin2019stop}, and transparency is a reason to publish them. Yet a decision tree published with the class counts in every leaf is also a small table of statistics about its training data, and statistics can be turned back into records. Census tables have been used to reconstruct the records of a whole country \citep{abowd2025census}, and reconstructed census rows can be ranked by how often they come back \citep{dick2023confidence}. For trees, \citet{ferry2024probabilistic} measure the uncertainty left over all datasets that fit one published tree, DRAFT \citep{ferry2024draft} reconstructs the training set of a random forest with constraint programming, and \citet{gorge2026dp} extend it to differentially private forests. \citet{hsu2026rashomon} show that publishing many near-optimal models from one Rashomon set, which TreeFARMS \citep{xin2022treefarms} enumerates for sparse trees, gives an attacker several views of the same data: their DRAFT-based error mostly falls as more trees are released.

Our two individual literature reviews looked at this work from different sides. The first (De Coster) compared what reconstruction attacks output. Most return one dataset: apart from the single-model analysis of \citet{ferry2024probabilistic}, none attaches a probability to each reconstructed value, and \citet{dick2023confidence} rank their rows but state that the ranking is not calibrated. The second (Fuligni) studied the attacker's background knowledge. DRAFT's experiments vary how many columns the attacker knows, not which ones \citep{ferry2024draft}, and the identity of a known attribute has only been studied for black-box attribute inference \citep{mehnaz2022sensitive}. Both reviews found the same evaluation weaknesses. Attacks on tree models are mostly compared with uniform guessing, while a baseline that knows the data distribution can change the conclusion \citep{abowd2025census,jayaraman2022imputation}, and the row matching used to score an anonymous reconstruction can use information the attacker does not have \citep{wen2025reconstruction}. Our shared starting point is therefore the Rashomon release of \citet{hsu2026rashomon} with a DRAFT-style attack and informed baselines, to which each of us added the open question from their own review. Code, data and results are available at \url{https://github.com/francescofuligni/rtds-project}.
