\begin{abstract}
% Student-authored abstract.
A decision tree published with its leaf class counts is also a set of statistics about
its training data. We release up to 200 near-optimal sparse trees from one Rashomon set,
trained on 100-row samples of binarized UCI Adult, and reconstruct each record's sex
with a DRAFT-based constraint-programming attack. Releasing more trees lowers the error from 34.6\%
to 25.3\% (always guessing male: 33.0\%). Background knowledge of marital status, education or age reduces the error by
about one point, while releasing random trees instead of the first trees in
enumeration order reduces it by six points. Using only the release and a public sample, prior-guided
sampling gives record-level scores whose mean is within about
two points of observed accuracy; at 50 trees it identifies 45\% of the records with 98\%
precision. With only 200 public records, confidence exceeds accuracy by 6--9 points;
a Dirichlet prior over record frequencies reduces this gap to about two points.
\end{abstract}
