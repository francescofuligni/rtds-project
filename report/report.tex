\begin{abstract}
% Student-authored abstract.
Releasing decision trees with leaf class counts reveals statistics about their training
records. We study up to 200 near-optimal trees trained on 100-row Adult samples,
combining two questions from our literature reviews: how known attributes affect sex
reconstruction, and whether an attacker can estimate which full-record claims are correct.
Reconstruction error falls mainly when more trees are released and when selection is
random rather than sequential. Background knowledge adds modest gains. Repeated solving
guided by public data gives confidence scores whose averages are close to observed accuracy. At 50 trees,
high-confidence claims cover 45\% of the sample with 97.6\% mean per-seed precision.
A Dirichlet prior reduces overconfidence when the public sample is small. These findings
show what average reconstruction error misses.
\end{abstract}
